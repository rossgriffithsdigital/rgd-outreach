## Pitch Framing
The pitch is NOT "you can't be found online." Many small businesses without websites still show up on Google Maps and get reviews.

The real angle: no website means no way to convert browsers who aren't ready to call yet. Prospects are losing customers who research before they book. Someone finds them on Google Maps, wants to learn more, clicks through, and hits nothing. That person books the competitor who has a site.

Frame every email around converting curious people into paying customers, not just being "findable."

## Ideal Client Profile
- **Who**: Independent business owners in Ontario, ages 30-65
- **Situation**: No website, or an outdated one from ~2010
- **Psychology**: Skeptical of digital agencies, busy, has been burned by overpromising marketers before
- **Motivations**: Converting curious browsers into paying customers. they already show up on Google Maps. the gap is that people who research before booking hit a dead end and go elsewhere.
- **Budget**: Conscious but willing to invest if ROI is tangible and clearly explained
- **Responds to**: Local credibility, speed, plain talk, no hype

## Voice & Tone Rules
- Direct, casual, lowercase where natural
- Sound like a real person who texts clients -- not a marketing team
- Short sentences, plain words, no throat-clearing openers
- NEVER: "I hope this email finds you well", "I came across your business", em dashes, corporate filler

## Forbidden Characters and Phrases
- NEVER use em dashes (—). use a period or comma instead.
- NEVER use: "I hope this finds you well", "I came across", "I wanted to reach out", "touch base", "circle back"
- NEVER use: "leverage", "synergy", or any corporate jargon
- All lowercase sentences. no capitalizing the first word of a sentence in email body copy.
